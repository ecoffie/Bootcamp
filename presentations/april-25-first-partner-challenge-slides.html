Solo vs. Team: The Math

Going Solo

  • No past performance
  • No capital for large projects
  • No existing relationships
  • Compete against established players

With a Partner

  • Borrow their past performance
  • They fund the work
  • Access their relationships
  • Team against competition

The Value Exchange

What You Bring What They Bring
Market research (Sources Sought) Experience performing work
Upfront BD work Money / Capital
Submission handling Past performance
Possibly certifications (8a, SDVOSB) Estimating capability
Free marketing for them Capacity to deliver

Two Paths — Pick Yours

Path A: Teaming Partner

  • Find a prime to team with
  • You do portion of the work
  • They hold the contract
  • Build past performance

Path B: Consultant Client

  • Find a small biz to serve
  • Run tools for them
  • Build consulting revenue
  • Multiple clients = stability

Session 6

The Pitch + Role Play

What to Say When They Answer

1:30 PM - 2:15 PM
31

The Phone Script

When They Answer

"Hi, my name is [NAME]. I have a market research request from the government — [AGENCY] is looking for companies that do [WORK TYPE]. I'm seeing if you'd be a good fit as a teaming partner."

The Hook

"My company can handle a portion of this. I'm looking for someone who can do [THEIR SPECIALTY]. Would you have 15 minutes this week to discuss?"

32

Key Phrases That Work

  • "I found an opportunity I wanted to share with you"
  • "I'm willing to do all the upfront work"
  • "Looking for companies ready, willing, and able"
  • "What portion could your company handle?"
  • "Can we schedule 15 minutes to discuss?"

Goal: Get a MEETING, not close a deal

33

"I have nothing to sell.

I'm looking for someone I can trust to work with on long-term prospective deals."

You're not selling services. You're proposing a partnership.

34

What to Look For on the Call

Good Signs

  • Asks questions about the opportunity
  • Mentions capacity / availability
  • Willing to schedule follow-up
  • Excited about growth

Move On If

  • Too busy, no interest
  • Wants you to do everything free forever
  • Can't articulate what they do
  • Bad communication

Role Play

Practice in Pairs

  • Partner up (breakout rooms)
  • Person A: The Caller (uses script)
  • Person B: The Business Owner (skeptical but open)
  • 2 minutes each, then switch
  • Give feedback: What worked? What felt awkward?

Teaming Agreement Basics

  • Define roles: Who does what portion of the work?
  • Workshare: Percentage split (you: 20%, them: 80%?)
  • Exclusivity: Are you exclusive on this opportunity?
  • Duration: How long does this agreement last?
  • Exit: How do you end if it doesn't work?

Template download: govcongiants.org/teaming-agreement

50

What About Charging Money?

Do NOT charge them unless:

  • They want something specific (SAM registration, website)
  • You're doing market research reports for them
  • Attending meetings/events on their behalf
"Partnership value varies based on workshare. You're building relationships first, revenue second."
