FACTS vs FACTION

Myths That Keep
You Out

Let's separate what's real from what's holding you back.

MYTH #1

Myth

"You need government experience to start"

Fact

Your commercial work counts.

Government buyers accept commercial past performance. If you've staffed a hospital, a warehouse, or a call center — you already have it.

MYTH #2

Myth

"It's all on SAM.gov"

Fact

Only 5% is publicly posted.

55% flows through task orders on contract vehicles. 30% goes sole-source. $86B in subcontracts never touches SAM.gov.

MYTH #3

Myth

"It takes years to win"

Fact

Subcontracting can start in weeks.

You don't need a prime contract to get started. Partner with a prime, deliver on a subcontract, and build past performance while getting paid.

What if one customer paid you
for 10, 20, 30 years?

Federal agencies don't churn like commercial clients.

Contracts renew. Task orders repeat. Relationships compound.

This is the "evergreen customer." The government is the most reliable, highest-paying client you'll ever have — once you get in.

NOW LET'S TALK ABOUT

How to Get
Started

The steps to position your company for federal contracts.

Define your core capability

Most staffing companies lose because they say "we do everything."

The government can't buy "staffing" — they buy specific services under specific codes.

Too broad

"We're a full-service staffing company providing talent across all industries nationwide."

Focused & findable

"We provide administrative support services (NAICS 561110) to federal agencies in the DC metro area."

Your formula: 1 role family + 1 NAICS code + 1 agency target = contracts you can actually win.

Your experience is enough

You think your experience isn't "federal enough" — you're wrong.

  • Staffed a hospital? That's healthcare support services past performance
  • Ran a call center? That's contact center services past performance
  • Filled warehouse shifts? That's logistics support services past performance
  • Placed security guards? That's physical security services past performance

The truth

Government buyers accept commercial past performance. You don't need prior federal work.

Who's actually buying?

AgencyWhat they buy (in staffing terms)
VANurses, admin backfills, call center reps, medical support
DoDWarehouse/logistics, base ops, admin support, security
DHSContact centers, ops support, mission surge staff
HHSProgram support, public-facing services, data entry
GSAFacilities maintenance, janitorial, building ops

These agencies already buy what you sell

They just won't find you if your website says "staffing" instead of "support services."

The benefits nobody talks about

  • Long-term contracts — 3-5 year base periods plus option years
  • Net-30 payment — the government always pays (they're the U.S. Treasury)
  • Set-aside advantages — small business certifications unlock non-competitive awards
  • Contract vehicles = pipeline — once you're on a vehicle, work comes to you

Compare that to commercial staffing: 60-90 day payment terms, client churn, constant rebidding. Government contracting is the most stable revenue stream in business.

The brutal challenges

Nobody posts this part on LinkedIn.

  • Compliance is real — SCA wage determinations, FAR clauses
  • Cash flow gap — you staff before you get paid (60+ days)
  • Proposal fatigue — government moves slow, demands detail
  • You will lose bids. A lot. — That's normal.

The question isn't whether it's hard. It's whether the payoff is worth it. (It is.)
