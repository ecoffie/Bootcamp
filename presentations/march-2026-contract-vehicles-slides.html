Contract Vehicles 101
The Big Picture

9:00 AM - 10:30 AM | 90 min 6

What is a Contract Vehicle?

A pre-approved contract that agencies can use to buy goods and services faster.

Without a Vehicle

  • Full & open competition
  • 60-180 day solicitation
  • Extensive proposal required
  • More compliance burden

With a Vehicle

  • Pre-qualified contractors
  • 14-30 day task orders
  • Streamlined proposals
  • Faster procurement

Task Orders: Where the Real Money Flows

  • Individual work packages under an IDIQ/GWAC
  • Typically 2-4 week response windows (not 60 days)
  • Shorter proposals - often 15-30 pages
  • "Fair opportunity" - all vehicle holders can compete
  • Some are directed to specific contractors (sole source)

Strategy: Get on a high-volume vehicle OR partner with someone who is.

12

Two Paths to Win Vehicle Work

Path 1: Get ON the Vehicle

  • Apply during on-ramp
  • Invest in getting schedule
  • Compete as prime on TOs
  • Higher margins
  • More admin burden

Path 2: Work THROUGH the Vehicle

  • Subcontract to a prime
  • No vehicle investment
  • Partner on task orders
  • Lower margins
  • Less admin, faster start

Both are valid strategies. We'll help you decide which is right for you.

OASIS+ (Professional Services)

What It Is

  • GSA's premier professional services vehicle
  • Replaced OASIS (2024)
  • All NAICS codes eligible

  • Categories (Large Categories A-Z)
  • Products AND services

Key Categories

  • IT (Category 54151)
  • Professional Services (541)
  • Facilities (561)
  • Office Products, Furniture

Note: Getting a GSA Schedule takes 6-12 months. Plan ahead!

What is a Sources Sought Notice?

A market research tool agencies use BEFORE writing the solicitation.

  • Agency is asking: "Who can do this work?"
  • Helps determine: Set-aside or full competition?
  • Your response shapes how the RFP is written
  • NOT a solicitation - no contract awarded yet

90% of contractors ignore Sources Sought. This is your chance to stand out.

Task Order
Response Strategy

2:45 PM - 4:00 PM | 75 min 37

Task Order vs. Open Market Proposals

Task Order

  • 2-4 week response
  • 15-30 page proposals
  • Pre-approved contractors
  • Pricing often pre-set
  • Faster award

Open Market

  • 30-60 day response
  • 50-200+ page proposals
  • Open competition
  • Full pricing required
  • Longer award timeline

Task orders = More opportunities, faster decisions, less proposal burden.

38

Task Order Response Framework

Technical Volume

  • Understanding of requirements
  • Technical approach
  • Management approach
  • Key personnel
  • Past performance

Pricing Volume

  • Labor rates (usually from vehicle)
  • Level of effort
  • ODCs and travel
  • Fee/margin
  • Total price

Key: Task orders often use vehicle labor rates. Know your rates before competing!

39

Fair Opportunity Process

  • All vehicle holders get chance to compete (usually)
  • Task order posted on vehicle portal or emailed
  • Q&A period for clarifications
  • Proposals evaluated, award made
  • Some exceptions allow directed awards

Exception: Under $7M, agencies can use "lowest price technically acceptable" or limit competition to a subset of vendors.

40

Teaming on Task Orders

As Prime

  • You're on the vehicle
  • Lead the proposal
  • Subcontract to partners
  • Full responsibility
  • Highest margin

As Subcontractor

  • Partner is on vehicle
  • Provide capabilities
  • Less proposal work
  • Lower risk
  • Lower margin

Both are valid. Choose based on your situation and the opportunity.

When to Get ON a Vehicle

  • High volume of task orders in your NAICS
  • Target agencies use the vehicle heavily
  • You have past performance to qualify
  • You can invest time and money (6-18 months)
  • You want to be prime and control relationships
  • On-ramp is open or opening soon
45

When to Work THROUGH a Partner

  • You're new to government contracting
  • Limited past performance - need to build it
  • Need revenue faster - can't wait 12+ months
  • Niche capability that primes need
  • Testing the market before committing
  • Vehicle is closed to new entrants
46

Finding Prime Partners

  • Check vehicle awardee lists (GSA, vehicle websites)
  • Filter by NAICS codes that match your capabilities
  • Research small business subcontracting plans
  • Look at past task order awards - who's winning?
  • Attend vehicle conferences and industry days
  • Use LinkedIn to connect with BD leads

Your Pitch to Primes

"I have [capability] and can help you win [type of work]. I'm certified [cert] which helps your SB goals."
