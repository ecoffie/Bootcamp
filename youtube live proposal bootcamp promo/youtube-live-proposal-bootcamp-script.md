## SLIDE 6: Why Most Task Order Proposals Fail
**The 5 Mistakes**

### Talking Points:
> "I've reviewed hundreds of proposals. And the same mistakes show up over and over.
>
> **Mistake #1: They copy-paste from full and open proposals.** Task order proposals are DIFFERENT. They're shorter. More focused. More specific to the task. You can't recycle your 200-page proposal and expect it to work.
>
> **Mistake #2: They don't read the evaluation criteria.** Every task order has scoring criteria. If the agency says 'technical approach is 60% of the score' and you spend 80% of your proposal on past performance, you already lost.
>
> **Mistake #3: They write about themselves instead of the mission.** Same problem I talked about with capability statements. The agency doesn't care about YOUR history. They care about THEIR problem. How are you going to solve it?
>
> **Mistake #4: They don't understand the pricing structure.** T&M, FFP, cost-plus — each one requires a different pricing strategy. If you price a T&M task like an FFP contract, you're either leaving money on the table or pricing yourself out.
>
> **Mistake #5: They submit late or non-compliant.** Task orders have tight deadlines. 7 to 14 days is normal. If you don't have your templates, your past performance narratives, and your team ready BEFORE the task order drops, you won't make the deadline."

**Transition:** "So what does a winning task order proposal actually look like?"

## SLIDE 7: Anatomy of a Winning Task Order Proposal
**The 4 Sections**

### Talking Points:
> "A winning task order proposal has four sections. And each one has a specific job.
>
> **Section 1: Technical Approach.** This is WHERE you win or lose. Don't describe your company. Describe your PLAN. How will you execute THIS specific task? What's your methodology? What are the deliverables? What are the milestones? Be specific to the Statement of Work.
>
> **Section 2: Staffing / Management Approach.** Who's doing the work? What are their qualifications? How is the team organized? Who's the project manager? What's the reporting structure? Agencies want to know you have REAL PEOPLE ready to go — not 'to be determined.'
>
> **Section 3: Past Performance.** Show work you've done that's SIMILAR to this task. Same size. Same scope. Same complexity. If you don't have federal past performance, commercial work counts — but you have to frame it correctly.
>
> **Section 4: Price / Cost.** Your pricing has to match the contract type. If it's T&M, you need loaded labor rates and hours. If it's FFP, you need a firm number with a cost breakdown. The price has to be realistic — too low and they don't trust you. Too high and you lose on cost.
>
> The bootcamp on February 28th? We're going through EACH of these sections. Live. With templates. You'll walk out with a proposal framework you can use on your next opportunity."

## SLIDE 9: Real Example — How Task Orders Actually Flow
**IDIQ → Task Order → Award — Visual Timeline**

### Talking Points:
> "Let me walk you through how this actually works in real life.
>
> Step 1: An agency — let's say the Department of Homeland Security — issues an IDIQ solicitation. They need IT modernization services over the next 5 years, ceiling of $500 million.
>
> Step 2: They evaluate proposals and award the IDIQ to, say, 8 companies. These 8 companies are now ON the vehicle. They have a 'hunting license.'
>
> Step 3: Over the next 5 years, DHS issues individual task orders under that IDIQ. 'We need a cloud migration for this office.' 'We need a cybersecurity assessment for that program.' Each task order is worth $500K, $2M, $10M.
>
> Step 4: Only those 8 companies can bid on each task order. Not 100 companies. Eight.
>
> Step 5: The companies submit short, focused proposals. The agency evaluates and awards — often within 30 to 60 days. Way faster than full and open.
>
> Now here's where you come in as a small business: You can be one of the 8. OR you can team with one of the 8 as a subcontractor. Either way, you're competing against 8 companies, not 80. That's the game."
