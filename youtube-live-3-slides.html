What the 71% Actually Means

The 71% breaks down into specific contract actions where no new competition occurred:

Contract Modifications & Options
Existing contracts renewed, extended, or modified. The incumbent keeps working. No new RFP issued. This is the largest chunk.
Task Orders on IDIQs
Work awarded under existing IDIQ vehicles (OASIS+, Alliant, SEWP, agency-level). Competition happened at the vehicle level — task orders flow to winners.
Sole Source Awards
Agency selects ONE vendor without competition. Justified by urgency, unique capability, or small business set-aside authority. 34% of all awards (GAO).

What the 71% Actually Means (continued)

These paths bypass traditional full & open competition entirely:

OTAs (Other Transactions)
Bypass FAR entirely. Used for R&D, prototyping, and rapid tech. $40B+ in DoD OTAs. Not on SAM.gov.
BPAs & GSA Orders
Blanket Purchase Agreements and GSA Schedule orders. Agencies buy from pre-approved lists. 3 quotes = award. No full & open.
BAAs & SBIR/STTR
Broad Agency Announcements for R&D. You submit a white paper, not an RFP response. SBIR/STTR reserved for small businesses.

Key insight: These aren't loopholes. They're built into the system to move faster than traditional procurement.

The Programs That Drive This

These 3 programs bypass traditional FAR-based competition:

1
OTAs (Other Transaction Authority) — $40B+ in DoD. Bypasses FAR. Used for prototyping & production. Enter through consortia like NSTXL, SOSSEC, NGA ATS.
2
BAAs (Broad Agency Announcements) — R&D funding. Submit a white paper, not a proposal. DoD, DARPA, IARPA, DOE all use them. Always open.
3
SBIR/STTR — $4B+/yr reserved for small businesses. Phase I ($50-250K), Phase II ($500K-1.5M), Phase III (unlimited, sole source).

GSA Schedule: The Quiet Competition Killer

Get on the list once. Receive orders for 20 years.

1
Get on GSA MAS once — 20-year contract. Application takes effort, but you only do it once.
2
Agencies buy from you directly — for orders under $25K, agencies can buy from a single Schedule holder. No competition.
3
$25K-$250K = 3 quotes — agency gets 3 quotes from Schedule holders. Not 50. Not 200. Three.

GSA Schedule: eBuy & BPAs

Once you're on Schedule, work comes to you:

4

How to Find Low-Competition Contracts

1
SAM.gov → filter "Sources Sought" — these are pre-RFP. Respond to shape the solicitation. Less than 5% do this.
2
SAM.gov → filter "Sole Source" — see what's being awarded non-competitively. Research who got it and why.
3
GSA eBuy — RFQs posted to Schedule holders. Quick-turn responses. If you're on Schedule, check eBuy weekly.
4
SBIR.gov — all open SBIR/STTR topics across every agency. Phase I = $50-250K. Submit a proposal, not an RFP response.
5
USASpending.gov → sub-agency search — find who's spending in your NAICS at the command level. Then call their SBLO.
6
Agency forecast pages — Google "[agency] procurement forecast." Most publish what they're buying next year. Get there 6-12 months early.
