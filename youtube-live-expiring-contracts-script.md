## SLIDE 5: Why Expiring Contracts Are Your Best Opportunity

Let me give you five reasons why expiring contracts are the best opportunities to pursue:

**Number one:** The work isn't going away. The agency still needs these services. They're required to rebid.

**Number two:** You know exactly what they're buying. The scope already exists. You can read the current contract and understand exactly what they need.

**Number three:** You know who has it now. You can research the incumbent. Study their approach. Identify their weaknesses.

**Number four:** You know WHEN it's coming. You can position 12 to 18 months early instead of scrambling when the solicitation drops.

**Number five:** Agencies WANT competition. They're actually required to look for alternatives. They want to hear from you.

This is the opposite of searching SAM.gov blindly. You know what, who, and when.

20% of $221 billion is $44 billion in opportunity going to someone who DIDN'T have the contract before.

The question isn't IF contracts change hands. They do — 20% of the time.

The question is whether YOU are positioned to be in that 20%.

## SLIDE 7: Two Ways to Win Expiring Contracts

There are two paths into this world.

**Path A: Compete Against the Incumbent**

Wait for the new solicitation. Submit a stronger proposal. Offer better value or innovation. Win the recompete outright.

This works best if you're an experienced firm with past performance in that exact area.

**Path B: Team WITH the Incumbent**

Contact the current contract holder NOW — before the recompete. Offer to sub on their recompete bid. Bring a capability they're missing. Build past performance for next time.

This is the path I recommend for newer contractors. You get on the contract, you build your record, and next time YOU can be the prime.

Start with Path B. Graduate to Path A.

---

## SLIDE 8: The 12–18 Month Sweet Spot

Here's the positioning timeline that separates winners from losers.

**18 months out:** Identify the contract. Research the incumbent. Understand the scope. This is pure research mode.

**12 months out:** Contact the agency. Respond to Sources Sought notices. Meet the Small Business Liaison Officer. Get on their radar.

**6 months out:** Build your team. Draft your capability statement specific to this contract. Approach the incumbent about teaming.

**When the solicitation drops:** You're already known. You're already positioned. You're already on a team or ready to compete. You submit and win.

Most contractors start at step 4 — when the solicitation drops. By then, it's too late. The teams are formed. The relationships are built.

Winners start at step 1.

## SLIDE 12: 3 Mistakes With Expiring Contracts

Let me tell you the three biggest mistakes I see.

**Mistake 1: Waiting for the solicitation to drop.**

By then, the incumbent and their team are already locked in. You're 18 months late to the game.

**Mistake 2: Ignoring the incumbent.**

They're not your enemy. They're your potential teaming partner. Contact them BEFORE the recompete. Many incumbents WANT small business partners to strengthen their proposal.

**Mistake 3: Not responding to Sources Sought.**

Less than 5% of contractors respond to Sources Sought notices. If you respond, the agency knows you exist. That matters when they're building their acquisition strategy.
