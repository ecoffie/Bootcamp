Thumbnail + Title Strategy
The thumbnail and title are two halves of one pitch. The thumbnail is the emotional hook — a big number, a bold claim, a visual that stops the scroll. The title adds context, specificity, and the reason to click. Together they answer: “What is this?” and “Why should I watch?”

Rule: Never repeat the same words in both. The viewer sees them side-by-side — if they say the same thing, you’ve wasted half your real estate.

How They Work Together
Stream 1: Thumbnail screams “SAM.gov is getting replaced” (controversy). Title adds mystery: “while the government was sleeping” (curiosity gap).
Stream 2: Thumbnail is just a massive $750B (number shock). Title explains what it is: a market nobody talks about (insider knowledge).
Stream 3: Thumbnail shows the feat: 24,000 contracts a night (scale). Title personifies it: an AI that never sleeps (character intrigue).
Stream 4: Thumbnail is the deadline: 9 DAYS (urgency). Title adds stakes: can we actually ship? (tension).
Stream 5: Thumbnail names the enemy: Lockheed, 47 people (David vs. Goliath). Title makes it tangible: $149/mo vs. an army (value prop).

Before You Post — Quick Notes
Above the fold: YouTube shows only the first ~150 characters before “Show more.” The opening line of every description is the hook. Don’t waste it on “Welcome to my channel.”
Links: getmindy.ai link is FIRST in every description. YouTube makes it clickable. This is your #1 conversion driver from streams.
Timestamps: Add after the stream ends. YouTube auto-generates chapters from timestamps, which boosts the VOD in search.
Tags: Included at the bottom of each description. Also add them in YouTube Studio’s tag field.
Pinned comment: Post immediately when going live. Pin it. This is your CTA for the live audience.
Category: Science & Technology
Visibility: Public. Schedule 15–30 min before start time so subscribers get notified.
