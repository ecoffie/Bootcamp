# First Partner Challenge - Reference Guide

**Source:** Previous 5-Day Challenge (proven format)
**Purpose:** Fast-track finding your first teaming partner

---

## The 5 Objectives

1. **Pick a lucrative industry** — NAICS, PSC, Keywords (max 2)
2. **Find Sources Sought in your industry** — Market opportunities
3. **Reach out to companies who can perform** — Build call list
4. **Have initial meeting with company** — Discovery call
5. **Form partnership and start working together** — Teaming agreement

---

## The Core Strategy

> "Get to step 3 as fast as possible because that's where the motion begins."

### The Value Exchange

| **What You Bring** | **What They Bring** |
|--------------------|---------------------|
| Market research (Sources Sought) | Experience performing the work |
| Upfront BD work | Money/capital |
| Submission handling | Past performance |
| Possibly certifications (8a, SDVOSB, etc.) | Estimating capability |
| Free marketing for them | Capacity to deliver |

### The Pitch (Not Selling Services)

> "I have a market research request from the government (ABC office) looking for companies that do this kind of work. I'm seeing if you'd be a good fit as a teaming partner. My company can do a portion if you can do the other portion."

**Key mindset:** "I have nothing to sell. I'm looking for someone to work with on long-term prospective deals."

## Day 1: Pick an Industry

### Why Just ONE Industry?
- Helps with market research
- Narrows down Keywords, NAICS, PSC codes
- Communicate with decision makers with singular focus
- Get to strategy faster: "I'm selling to THIS office" not "I'm selling to the government"

### Rules
- Maximum 2 NAICS, PSC, or Keywords
- Everyone should know you for ONE thing
- Double niche down: Not "Construction" → "HVAC Construction" or "Roofing"

### How to Pick
Pick based on one of three reasons:
1. **Current strong connections**
2. **Past work experience**
3. **Plenty of money being spent**

## Day 2: Find Sources Sought

### What is a Sources Sought?
A government market research notice (FAR 15.201) where agencies seek information about vendor capabilities before issuing a formal solicitation.

### Why Sources Sought?

**Value to You:**
- Information on NEW requirements (not previously listed)
- Not widely publicized/searchable
- Demonstrate understanding and value

**Value to Your Partner:**
- Early opportunity identification
- Influence solicitation requirements
- Visibility with procurement officers
- Understand competition
- Technical insight into requirements

### How to Find Sources Sought
1. **SAM.gov** — Filter by "Sources Sought" notice type
2. **Agency Websites** — Procurement/acquisition sections
3. **Market Assassin** — Automated search by NAICS

### What if No Sources Sought?
- Find an old one (still valid for outreach)
- Find an RFP instead
- Choose another industry temporarily

### How to Respond
- Letter of Interest
- Capability Statement

## Day 3: Reach Out to Companies

### Why Do You Need Other Companies?
- They have the **experience** you need
- They have the **money** you need
- Together you can win contracts neither could alone

### Where to Find Companies

**Primary:**
- SAM.gov entity search
- DSBS (Dynamic Small Business Search)
- DoD $7M Awards List
- Federal Contractor Database (GovCon Giants tool)

**Secondary (10 Places):**
- Facebook groups
- LinkedIn forums
- Industry-specific events
- Local small business conferences
- Online webinars
- City/State small business lists
- Chambers of Commerce
- Professional associations (EO, YPO)
- Charity events
- FederalHelpCenter

### Building Your Call List
- Put in Google Sheet with: Phone, Email, Address, Specialty, POC
- Align with Sources Sought requirements
- Call EVERYONE — "Call to learn, not to earn"

### Who to Talk To
**Target:** Owner or Decision Maker
**Avoid:** Business development/sales people (they see you as competition)
**If unavailable:** Leave message for owner

## Day 4: Initial Meeting

### Goal of the Call
- **Discovery** — Learn what they need
- **Get consent** for a longer meeting

### What to Say (Phone Script)

1. Introduce yourself nicely
2. "I have a market research request from the government (ABC office) looking for companies like yours that do this kind of work."
3. "I'm seeing if you'd be a good fit as a teaming partner to help complete this request."
4. "My company can do a portion if you can do the other portion."
5. "What portion can you do?" (Reason for follow-up call)
6. Go over requirements together
7. Ask if you can talk again to discuss work share

**Key phrases:**
- "I would be willing to do all the upfront work and handle submitting the request on our behalf."
- "I'm looking for companies ready, willing, and able to do this type of work."

**Objective:** Get off the phone with a MEETING scheduled, not close the deal.

### What to Look For
- Willingness to work together
- Excited about growth opportunities
- Clear communication about capabilities

### The Pitch (Not Services — Partnership)

> "I have nothing to sell. I'm looking for someone I can trust to work with on long-term prospective deals. I understand this is a long game and I'm here for the journey. Everything I read says: if you want to go fast, go alone — if you want to go far, go together."

**Benefits for Them:**
- Free marketing/BD
- Someone finding opportunities for them
- Possibly your certifications

**Benefits for You:**
- Partner with experience and money
- Free estimator
- Someone who can perform the work

## Day 5: Form Partnership

### Next Steps After Agreement
1. Signed teaming agreement
2. Define roles and responsibilities
3. Joint capability statement

### Charging Money
**Do NOT charge unless:**
- They want something specific (SAM registration, DSBS, website)
- Market research reports
- Attending meetings/events on their behalf

Partnership value varies based on work share and responsibilities.

### Daily Activities After Partnership
1. Market research (ongoing)
2. Build org charts for target agencies
3. Respond to Sources Sought together
4. Capability briefings
5. RFP/RFQ responses
6. Communicate with partner weekly/biweekly

### Continue Building Pipeline
- Talk to more people
- Learn about more opportunities
- Identify additional qualifications/certifications needed
- Build network of subcontractors and other partners
